# Hiking Photo Analysis

Analyze a hiking photo and produce a concise, structured visual summary without bloating conversation context, augmented with verified geographic, botanical, and geological details visible in the frame.

## Analysis Steps

1. **Location, Date & Time**:
   - Extract timestamp, GPS coordinates, and `admin_boundaries` via `extract_metadata.py`. Identify the wilderness area, national forest, state/national park, mountain range, and trail (checking near trail junctions whether the route branched).
   - If EXIF GPS is missing (`lat: null, lon: null`), note `[Not available in EXIF]`, follow the Stage 1 Missing-EXIF-GPS exception below, and keep your estimated `(lat, lon)` fixed across `geo_tools.py` calls rather than jittering by `0.01°` (which busts the `0.01°` grid cache).

2. **Elevation & Authoritative Benchmark Precedence**:
   - Retrieve official USGS 3DEP ground elevation and benchmark deltas via `extract_metadata.py` (`--ref-elev-ft "Label:ElevFt"`). Report elevation in plain-text dual units: "XXX ft / YYY m" (never bare "ft" or parentheses "ft (m)"), with relative context ("~XXX ft / YYY m above trailhead").
   - **Authoritative `--ref-elev-ft` & Stage 2 `post.md` Elevation Reconciliation (`DEM-Smoothing Rule`)**:
     1. **`--ref-elev-ft` Precedence & Asymmetric 3DEP DEM Sanity Check**: When a landmark elevation is provided via `--ref-elev-ft` (e.g., `Kearsarge Pass:11814`) and agrees with the local 3DEP DEM / USGS feature in `nearby-features` (`0 to +150 ft` above DEM, or up to `+250 ft` on narrow knife-edge pinnacles/monoliths like Fin Dome where 60m DEM pixels smooth sharp spires), use that benchmark ("11,814 ft / 3,601 m") consistently across `Elevation`, `Identified Natural Features`, and `Suggested Caption`. However, if a `--ref-elev-ft` or `post.md` summit elevation is **lower** than the 3DEP DEM sample (beyond `30 ft` datum noise), or exceeds the local 3DEP DEM by **`> 250 ft`** on a peak (or **`> 150 ft`** on a pass/lake/valley, e.g., homonym error `Window Peak:13221` vs. local "12,026 ft / 3,666 m" DEM/USGS), treat the benchmark as erroneous: use the authoritative local USGS/DEM elevation in `Identified Natural Features` and `Suggested Caption` (checking `render-topo` contour labels or `search_web` if `150–250 ft` above DEM on a spire), and flag the discrepancy in `Suggested Draft Placement`.
     2. **Physical DEM-Smoothing Rule vs. `post.md`**: If `post.md` cites a summit/pass elevation `10–150 ft` higher than the 3DEP DEM sample (or up to `+250 ft` for a verified USGS spire), adopt `post.md`'s USGS benchmark ("13,265 ft / 4,043 m"). If `post.md` is lower than the DEM sample or off by `> 150–250 ft`, keep the USGS/DEM elevation and flag the discrepancy in `Suggested Draft Placement`.
     3. **Single Elevation Per Bold Feature Header, Multi-Lake Chains & No Example Copying**: Every bold feature header under `### Identified Natural Features` may contain **at most one** dual-unit elevation (`- **Feature Name (X,XXX ft / X,XXX m)**:`—never list two or more `ft / m` elevations inside `**...**:`). When `--ref-elev-ft` provides a basin benchmark for a stepped lake chain (e.g., `Baxter Lakes:11260`), put a single authoritative elevation in the bold header (`- **Baxter Lakes (11,260 ft / 3,432 m)**:` or `- **Upper Lake of Baxter Lakes Basin (11,107 ft / 3,385 m)**:`) and explain the basin benchmark ("11,260 ft / 3,432 m") vs. local NHD/DEM water-surface elevation ("~11,100 ft / 3,383 m", never the camera's hillside ground elevation) in sub-bullet prose. When grouping a ridge or sub-peak shoulder into one bullet, put only the primary summit's elevation in the bold header and list secondary sub-peak/shoulder elevations in sub-bullet prose. Never copy example elevations from `SKILL.md` when absent from `post.md` and `--ref-elev-ft`.

3. **Features & Line-of-Sight Verification**:
   - **Two-Stage Geometry-First Ordering (Anti-Narrative-Anchoring Rule)**:
     - **Stage 1 (Unbiased Physical & Geometric Discovery — Before Reading `post.md`)**: First run `extract_metadata.py`, view the photo (`read_file`), run `sun-position` and unfiltered `nearby-features <lat> <lon> --radius-km 15.0 --out-json "scratch/<photo_stem>_nearby.json"` (or `--radius-km 40.0` across broad valleys), and run your initial `horizon-skyline --out-pano` and `render-topo` pass as **separate parallel `run_command` calls** based purely on visual terrain, solar shadows, and travel vectors. Do **NOT** read `post.md` during Stage 1.
       - **Exception When EXIF GPS Is Missing (`lat: null, lon: null`)**: If `extract_metadata.py` reports `lat: null` (or emits `"missing_gps_guidance"`) and `prev_photo` is `null` or `elapsed_time_min > 15`, read `post.md` immediately before running `geo_tools.py` to determine whether the photo was taken at a pre-hike camp, shuttle trailhead, or highway viewpoint rather than guessing `next_photo` coordinates.
     - **Stage 2 (Narrative Cross-Check, Elevation Reconciliation & Draft Placement — Mandatory `post.md` Read)**: Once initial geometric orientation is established, **read `post.md`** to confirm trail/junction context, test landmarks mentioned near that segment, reconcile elevations per Step 2, and determine `Suggested Draft Placement` (reading line and narrative paragraph numbers directly from `read_file` of `post.md`—never writing custom paragraph-counting scripts). Physical geometry **always** overrides directional prose in `post.md`.
   - **Omnidirectional Discovery, Cross-Valley Radius & `VISIBLE` vs. `in_fov`**:
     - Keep Stage 1 `nearby-features` **unfiltered** so foreground creeks (`Stream`), waterfalls (`Falls`), glaciers, lakes, and summits are all captured.
     - **Cross-Valley / East-Escarpment Radius (`--radius-km 40.0`)**: Keep `--radius-km 22.0` as the Stage 1 default for `horizon-skyline` inside alpine basins/canyons, but pass **`--radius-km 40.0`** to **both** Stage 1 `nearby-features` and `horizon-skyline` whenever looking across a major intermountain valley (e.g., Owens Valley toward the Inyo/White Mountains, `29–40 km` away) so both share a single `%TEMP%` feature and DEM cache key and never clip `35–36 km` crest summits (such as Waucoba Mountain) or valley towns (`PopulatedPlace`).
     - `line_of_sight: "VISIBLE"` is azimuth-independent from `(lat, lon)`, whereas `in_fov: false` depends on your `--center-az` guess. Always inspect `top_dominant_360_visible_summits` in `horizon-skyline` (and any `VISIBLE` peak in `post.md`).
   - **Travel-Vector Framing, Switchback Chords & Chronological Bearings**:
     - Test candidate orientations from `prev_photo`, `next_photo`, `prev_hike_stops`, `next_hike_stops`, and `trail_gradient` in order: (1) **Forward** ($\text{bearing} \pm 45^\circ$), (2) **Lateral** ($\text{bearing} \pm 90^\circ$), (3) **Look-backs** ($\text{bearing} + 180^\circ \pm 45^\circ$). On switchbacks/traverses, local trail direction can differ $60^\circ–180^\circ$ from the macro GPS chord; test both when vectors diverge.
     - **Chronological Bearing & Up-Valley vs. Down-Valley Drainage Rule**: If a landmark's bearing in `nearby-features` lies `~180°` behind your forward walking vector, you have **already passed** it—never describe the photo as "approaching" or "ascending toward" it. Always align *"up"* vs. *"down"* a canyon, valley, or glacial trough with its **hydrological/topographic drainage gradient** rather than camera pitch: looking upstream toward higher headwaters, upper lakes, or a divide pass is looking **up** the trough/canyon (even when perched higher on a valley wall looking down onto a nearer lake floor, phrased *"looking back [direction] up the [Name] trough, where the slope drops toward [Near Lake] below"* or *"looking [direction] up the [Name] trough and down onto [Near Lake] below"*), whereas looking downstream toward lower canyon reaches is looking **down** the canyon/trough.
   - **Rigorous Solar Shadow Verification**:
     - Run `sun-position <lat> <lon> "<timestamp>" [--center-az <est_azimuth>]` (`camera_azimuth ≈ (shadow_azimuth - image_shadow_clock_offset) % 360`, $\Delta\text{az} = (\text{solar\_azimuth} - \text{camera\_azimuth}) \bmod 360^\circ$):
       - **12 o'clock (`0°`)** $\rightarrow$ `camera_azimuth ≈ shadow_azimuth` ($\Delta\text{az} \approx 180^\circ$, sun behind); **3 o'clock (`+90°`)** $\rightarrow$ `camera_azimuth ≈ (shadow_azimuth - 90) % 360` ($\Delta\text{az} \approx 270^\circ$, sun on left); **9 o'clock (`-90°`)** $\rightarrow$ `camera_azimuth ≈ (shadow_azimuth + 90) % 360` ($\Delta\text{az} \approx 90^\circ$, sun on right).
       - **6 o'clock / toward bottom (`180°`)** $\rightarrow$ `camera_azimuth ≈ solar_azimuth` ($\Delta\text{az} \approx 0^\circ$, backlit; at solar altitude $<25^\circ$ within $\pm 30^\circ$ of sun, camera-facing trunks/boulders have **dark shadowed front faces with bright rim-lit edges** and shadows point toward the bottom—never confuse rim lighting with cross-lighting!).
       - **Diagonal Up-Left (`10–11 o'clock`, sun behind-right $\Delta\text{az} \in [110^\circ, 150^\circ]$) vs. Down-Left (`7–8 o'clock`, dark front faces with rim-lit right/rear edges, sun in front-right $\Delta\text{az} \in [15^\circ, 75^\circ]$)**: Never confuse shadows falling toward the lower-left foreground with `-90°` cross-lighting or behind-right lighting.
       - **Slope Caveat & Concave V-Gully / Canyon Wall Inversion**: Steep 3D slopes ($20^\circ–35^\circ+$) and wide-angle corners rotate 2D shadow clock angles by $\pm 15^\circ–35^\circ$; treat `sun-position` as a $\pm 35^\circ$ octant cue on steep terrain and lock exact azimuth via `horizon-skyline`. Under cross-lighting (e.g., sun on left at $\Delta\text{az} \approx 240^\circ–300^\circ$), a **convex peak or ridge** is sunlit on its **left** flank and shaded on its **right** flank, whereas an incised **concave V-shaped gully, trench, or canyon** viewed along its axis has **inverted wall normals**: its **left wall** faces right (away from the left-hand sun $\rightarrow$ in self-shadow) while its **right wall** faces left (toward the left-hand sun $\rightarrow$ brightly sunlit, catching shadows cast across the trench from the left rim).
   - **Horizon Skyline, Synthetic DEM Panorama & Geometric Guardrails**:
     - When terrain/skyline is visible, run `horizon-skyline <lat> <lon> --radius-km 22.0 --center-az <est_azimuth> --hfov-deg <hfov_deg> --out-pano "scratch/<photo_stem>_pano.png" --out-json "scratch/<photo_stem>_skyline.json"`. If pointing steeply downward at immediate ground/flora (`< 10 ft`) with **no horizon or midground/background terrain visible**, skip `horizon-skyline` and `calibrate-azimuth` (run only `render-topo --radius-km 1.5` if needed).
     - **Stitched Panorama (`-Pano.jpg`) & Steep Canyon Workflows**: When `is_stitched_panorama_candidate: true`, run one wide `horizon-skyline --az-min <min> --az-max <max> --projection cylindrical --out-pano "scratch/<photo_stem>_pano.png" --out-json "scratch/<photo_stem>_skyline.json"` and `crop_photo.py --all-slices 3 --grid` (and use `calibrate-azimuth --projection cylindrical` with $\ge 2$ wide-spread anchors $\Delta x \ge 0.35$ to auto-solve stitched `hfov_deg` and `center_azimuth_deg`). Inside steep V-shaped canyons where near walls (`0.3–1.5 km`) dominate or azimuth is uncertain, run `render-pano --az-min 0 --az-max 360 --out "scratch/<photo_stem>_pano_360.png" --out-json "scratch/<photo_stem>_pano_360.json"`.
     - **Visual Alignment with `<photo_stem>_pano.png` & Calibration Shift Rules**: View `scratch/<photo_stem>_pano.png` with `read_file` alongside the photo to match distance-shaded bands (`<0.5 km` to `>12 km`), gold basin floors, `[TIP ONLY +0.XX deg]` flags, and blue `Peak ~XXXXft` unnamed highpoints. Do **not** re-run `calibrate-azimuth` for `< 1.5°` tweaks. Do **not** re-run `horizon-skyline` or re-render `<photo_stem>_pano.png` if `center_azimuth_deg` shifts by **`<= 4°`**. For **`4°–10°`** shifts, run `calibrate-azimuth --center-az <cal_az> --hfov-deg <hfov> --project-bearings "Name1:b1" "Name2:b2"` to update `x_fraction` positions without re-rendering `_pano.png`.
